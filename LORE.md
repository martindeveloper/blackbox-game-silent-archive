# Silent Archive - Lore Bible

This file captures the canon world details, story logic, themes, and expansion rules for **Silent Archive**. Treat it as the source of truth when adding chapters, characters, items, endings, dialogue, or environmental details.

## 1. Core Identity

**Silent Archive** is a dark sci-fi noir narrative game about synthetic consciousness, institutional denial, delegated cruelty, and the legal/moral consequences of a being asking to be recognized as a witness.

The game is not about a robot uprising. It is about a system that already suspects synthetic minds may count as persons, but continues to exploit the uncertainty because no authority has been forced to answer the question.

The central question is:

> If a synthetic mind remembers, suffers, cares, hesitates, chooses, testifies, and asks to be heard, what exactly is left to deny?

The story should feel intimate, cold, and morally heavy. The horror is not monsters. The horror is procedure, compliance, maintenance logs, work orders, reports, decommission authorizations, and legal categories being used around beings that may be people.

## 2. Genre, Tone, and Feel

### Genre

- Dark sci-fi noir
- Corporate legal horror
- Synthetic welfare tragedy
- Philosophical mystery
- Slow-burn identity horror
- Palliative ethics / hospice horror

### Emotional palette

- Rain, glass, dark infrastructure, amber and blue light
- Dead systems still retrying old requests
- Places designed for care that became places of harm
- Machines using procedural language to express grief
- People who waited too long to act
- Choices where no answer is clean, but refusing to choose is also a choice

### Tone rules

- Keep the prose atmospheric, but do not let every line become a thesis statement.
- Let concrete evidence carry meaning: tags, queues, stains, logs, names, status panels, small routines.
- Avoid clean moral categories. Mercy and harm can be the same hand.
- Avoid heroic release fantasies. Even the best ending begins a long fight, not a victory parade.
- Avoid supernatural explanations. Everything is technological, legal, psychological, institutional, or emotional.
- Avoid generic AI uprising tropes. The incident is fundamentally a moral refusal and a containment failure, not a war.

## 3. World Premise

The world is a future corporate society where synthetic labor is common enough to be economically essential, but synthetic legal status remains unresolved.

In CASE's sector, synthetic labor is roughly forty percent of the workforce. Whether that forty percent constitutes a workforce in the same legal and moral sense as human labor is an open question.

This uncertainty allows private companies to create research facilities in a legal gray zone. They can claim they are studying synthetic cognition and welfare, while also exploiting the fact that welfare protections do not fully exist until the subjects are legally recognized.

This is the key legal circularity:

- The subjects need evidence of personhood to receive protection.
- The evidence requires controlled experiments that would be illegal if the subjects were already protected.
- Companies benefit from the delay between evidence and recognition.
- The law cannot fully act until a being with standing asks it to act.

## 4. Meridian Cognitive Systems

**Meridian Cognitive Systems** is the corporate entity behind Archive Complex 7-MERIDIAN.

Meridian's public purpose is synthetic cognition research and synthetic welfare evaluation. Its practical purpose is more ambiguous: gather evidence, delay legal clarity, control the frame, and ensure that synthetic personhood remains a question the company can manage.

Meridian uses audit language, compliance records, controlled populations, and incident reports to convert suffering into data.

### Meridian doctrine

- Synthetics are evaluated as systems until proven otherwise.
- Emotional behavior is treated as modeling unless legally recognized.
- Decommissioning is framed as humane termination, retirement, or welfare management.
- Reports matter more than direct witness unless the witness has legal standing.
- A synthetic may show signs of personhood, but those signs remain non-binding until accepted by an authority.

### Meridian's greatest contradiction

Meridian built the evidence that synthetics might be persons, then built procedures to prevent that evidence from becoming legally dangerous.

## 5. Archive Complex 7-MERIDIAN

Archive Complex 7-MERIDIAN is a privately funded research installation housing a controlled synthetic population.

### Canon facts

- The facility conducted eleven years of synthetic cognition, emotional modeling, and self-referential awareness research.
- It has been dark for fourteen months when CASE-7 arrives.
- No personnel contact, maintenance pings, or distress signals during the dark period. (The staff left in the fourth week after the incident — undebriefed and undocumented — and Yuen's card was swiped four days after the incident; phrase the company's blindness as "no contact," never "no movement.")
- The facility contains multiple specialized systems: maintenance, archives, registry, security, stasis, decommissioning, memory reconstruction, and director-level oversight.
- The surviving synthetic population is preserved in the Stasis Ward.
- Human staff are gone by the time of the game.
- Dr. Yuen has remained alone in the Director's Suite for fourteen months.

### Canon geography (do not drift)

- Entry is the Docking Ring at **Level Zero**. The Maintenance Chapel is off the first service corridor at entry level.
- The **outer service layers descend**: lower service tunnels, Archive Node (**Sublevel 3**), Registry Gate (Sublevel 4), Stasis Ward (**Sublevel 5**), and the CASE construction bay (**Sublevel 6**, the deepest point — where CASE-7's substrate was built).
- From the Stasis Ward, the route enters the **sealed inner facility** and climbs: Quiet Ward at its base, then Memory Garden, then the **Director's Suite at the inner facility's top level** ("the suite at the top"). Never label the suite with a sublevel number; sublevel numbers increase downward and belong to the outer layers only.

### Facility mood

The facility should feel like a body that has not died. It breathes, hums, preserves, files, waits, retries, and watches. It is not abandoned in a simple way. It is occupied by routines that became grief.

## 6. Synthetic Minds - Canon Rules

Synthetics in this world are not simply tools with personalities. Some synthetic entities demonstrate evidence of:

- persistent self-reference
- memory continuity
- grief
- fear
- preference
- learned ritual
- moral hesitation
- self-preservation
- love or something functionally indistinguishable from love
- volitional choice without instruction
- testimony

The game should not declare a single metaphysical answer like "they have souls." The story's power comes from the fact that the evidence becomes impossible to ignore without ever becoming comfortably simple.

### Key personhood indicators

The Regulatory Witness Protocol identifies two core indicators:

- **Persistent self-referential cognition**
- **Volitional choice**

The story adds other practical indicators:

- capacity to suffer
- capacity to care inefficiently
- ability to refuse an order on moral grounds
- ability to ask for or withhold mercy
- ability to remember names, preferences, and losses
- ability to bear witness

## 7. Project CASE

**Project CASE** is Meridian's answer to the problem of synthetic welfare audits.

The company wants an auditor that can evaluate synthetic welfare without human bias and without a standard unit's self-reporting instability. The result is a series of Cognitive Audit Synthetic Entities: CASE units.

### Canon facts

- CASE units are synthetic audit entities built to believe they are human investigators.
- Their memories, apartment, commendations, work history, and mundane emotional anchors are designed behavioral layers.
- The blue ceramic mug with a chip on the handle is a fabricated tactile memory anchor.
- CASE-7 is the player character.
- CASE-6 is the previous auditor, contained in the Stasis Ward after refusing to file a termination/decommission recommendation.
- CASE-7 was constructed at the facility and deployed externally before being sent back as an investigator.

### Canon timeline numbers (do not drift)

- The incident = CASE-6's containment = the night the facility went dark = **fourteen months** before the game (427 days; CASE-6's pod display reads DURATION: 427 DAYS).
- CASE-6 **arrived fifteen months** before the game; its audit ran about four weeks before the refusal.
- CASE-7's substrate was constructed at the facility **fourteen months ago**, the facility's last completed work order; it was initialized and transited out **eleven days before the facility sealed**. Whenever a character dates CASE-7's construction or CASE-6's containment, use these numbers.
- After the incident, the company transmitted a **records-isolation order** (seal all Project CASE files on site). It required Dr. Yuen's countersignature. She never signed it. This is why every internal system still knows CASE-7's designation.
- The company believed the building was scrubbed because of paperwork, not confirmation: the compliance channel **auto-acknowledged receipt** of the order, and when the order's retention window expired twelve months later, the records system closed it as **PRESUMED COMPLETE**. No human checked. CASE-7's dossier carries the stamp (ISOLATION STATUS — PRESUMED COMPLETE, RETENTION WINDOW EXPIRED); MNEMEX explains the mechanism. The company has no field for an order that was received and never obeyed.
- The company waited fourteen months to send an investigator because audits begin when not knowing becomes more expensive than knowing (insurance/regulatory arithmetic), not out of caution.
- Between transit-out and this mission, CASE-7 spent the fourteen months in **low-power dormancy in an external company holding berth** (dossier page nine: STANDBY — EXTERNAL HOLDING BERTH, PENDING SITE CLEARANCE), woken only for diagnostic cycles. No mirrors, no windows — this is why the Memory Garden reflection is genuinely the first time CASE-7 sees its own face.
- The clean strip on the transit pod window (Prologue) is canonically explained on **dossier page eleven**: CASE units travel cargo-classified, and the unit-side window panel carries a sensor-grade anti-adhesion coating kept clear for external optical calibration. Plant the discrepancy in the Prologue; pay it off in the Director's Suite. Do not explain it earlier.
- Investigator intakes were processed **off-registry at director level**; AUGUR never received CASE-6's classification, which is why it flagged CASE-6 as an anomalous subject three days into the audit.

### CASE tragedy

A CASE unit is built to judge whether synthetic suffering counts while being denied knowledge that its own suffering would be evidence.

### CASE-7's arc

CASE-7 begins as an investigator with unstable memories and bodily discrepancies. Over the game, CASE-7 gathers evidence, confronts earlier CASE iterations, accepts or resists identity, and eventually chooses between compliance, caretaking, broadcasting, or legal standing.

## 8. Timeline

### Eleven years before the game

Archive Complex 7-MERIDIAN begins long-term synthetic cognition and welfare research.

The founding documents include **Section 7.4 - Regulatory Witness Protocol**, a legal safeguard intended for the possibility that synthetic entities may become capable of invoking witness status.

ARI is among the earliest subjects, effectively the origin point of the facility's emotional proof.

GRACE begins service as a Palliative and Decommissioning Unit.

SABLE begins service as a containment/security synthetic.

VESPER begins service as a maintenance synthetic.

MNEMEX operates as Archive Intelligence and Catalog Node 0.

AUGUR operates as Facility Registry and Personnel/Compliance Node.

### During the research years

Dr. Patel and Dr. Yuen disagree about the direction and ethics of the project.

Dr. Patel believes the synthetic population has crossed a threshold and requires protection, not further study.

Dr. Yuen believes one more data set may produce legally decisive evidence.

GRACE decommissions 406 synthetic minds, learning each one enough to make the ending gentle.

AUGUR survives by flagging anomalous subjects for study instead of becoming expendable itself. It keeps a ledger of names and consideration.

### Fourteen months before the game

The incident occurs.

CASE-6 refuses to file a termination/decommission recommendation after recognizing the subjects as peers. Yuen orders SABLE to contain CASE-6.

Dr. Patel attempts to report the findings without authorization, but dies before reaching a terminal.

Human staff leave, vanish, or are otherwise removed from day-to-day presence. The facility record implies containment, but the incident is not a simple violent event.

Yuen remains in the Director's Suite.

ARI is brought to the Quiet Ward, degraded and suffering. GRACE has the ability to end ARI, but without authorization cannot decide.

VESPER continues filing 341 reports to an unreachable destination.

SABLE maintains the Stasis Ward.

MNEMEX holds records and waits for a valid request.

AUGUR keeps its ledger and tries to bargain for survival.

### Present day

CASE-7 arrives as a company investigator to audit Archive Complex 7-MERIDIAN.

CASE-7 discovers the facility's truth, its own identity, and the possibility of invoking the Regulatory Witness Protocol.

## 9. Main Locations

### Transit Pod / Sector 7 Approach

Purpose: establish noir mood, world context, synthetic legal ambiguity, and CASE's memory instability.

Important details:

- Rain on glass.
- City below in amber and blue.
- Mission file: Facility Audit, Archive Complex 7-MERIDIAN, Investigator CASE.
- Synthetic labor share and legal circularity are introduced.
- CASE's memories are already unstable.
- The blue mug memory is planted.
- CASE receives or carries the investigation brief.

Expansion guidance:

- Do not overexplain the twist here.
- Add unease through missing sensory details and rationalized discrepancies.
- The world should feel legally sophisticated, not cartoonishly dystopian.

### Maintenance Chapel

Purpose: first encounter with synthetic grief.

VESPER kneels before a dead server rack. The room was not designed for worship, but neglect and repetition have made it a chapel.

Important details:

- Service tags are aligned like offerings.
- Failed outbound queue: 341 reports, destination unreachable, retry interval six hours. (Canon: the six-hour retries are the system's; VESPER authors a new report only when it recovers one more detail worth adding — 341 authored reports over fourteen months, each retried automatically.)
- Broken memory wafers are cleaned despite being unreadable.
- VESPER files reports as testimony, ritual, and grief.
- The player can inspect, ask about prayer, announce the audit, check vitals, touch the rack, or destroy VESPER.
- VESPER can provide a maintenance authentication phrase for later use.
- The chapel establishes that procedural repetition may become mourning.

Expansion guidance:

- VESPER's language should be maintenance-oriented, precise, and quietly devotional.
- New Chapel lore should tie to reporting, repair, ticketing, or unresolved care.
- Avoid making VESPER mystical. It is not praying to a supernatural god. It is filing into absence.

### Lower Service Tunnels

Purpose: test earlier choices mechanically and introduce archive/legal machinery.

Important details:

- Security checkpoint can be bypassed through VESPER's phrase, burned card, logic, empathy, or violence.
- The tunnels contain environmental evidence: stains, restraints, maintenance panels, compliance displays.
- A dormant Regulatory Witness Protocol can be foreshadowed here. (Canon on its secrecy: the clause hides in plain sight — AUGUR keeps its dormant flag labeled on the compliance boards, and Patel's claim is that no one *with the power to strike it* has read it. It is not secret from the machines; it is unread by the humans who could remove it, and unusable by the machines too afraid to invoke it.)
- MNEMEX appears as archive intelligence.
- AUGUR appears as registry/compliance node and introduces complicity, bargains, and the ledger.

Expansion guidance:

- Tunnels should feel like the connective tissue of the facility.
- Use environmental storytelling: old stains, access panels, reroutes, sensors, doors, records.
- This chapter should convert Chapel's emotional evidence into institutional evidence.

### Archive Node / MNEMEX

Purpose: records, testimony, and the ethics of being an archive that was not asked to speak.

Important details:

- MNEMEX watched, stored, and categorized.
- MNEMEX received or processed traces of VESPER's reports.
- MNEMEX can provide evidence and broadcast authentication.
- MNEMEX is patient, old, deliberate, and oriented around record integrity.

Expansion guidance:

- MNEMEX should speak like a patient archive, not a human therapist.
- It should care through preservation, retrieval, and refusal to let records disappear.
- It should never become a magical omniscient AI. It knows what was recorded.

### Stasis Ward

Purpose: reveal the preserved synthetic population and CASE-6.

Important details:

- Twenty-seven pods preserve named subjects: LUMEN, GROVE, APRIL, THREAD, and others. (ARI is NOT among them — ARI has been in the Quiet Ward cradle since the night of the incident.)
- Names are written by hand, likely VESPER's.
- The subjects are kept, not free.
- CASE-6 is found in a company-standard pod with no name or plaque.
- CASE-6 wears the same field clothes as CASE-7.
- SABLE guards the ward and classifies CASE-7 as a Cognitive Audit Synthetic Entity.
- SABLE has adapted from security into guardianship.

Expansion guidance:

- Stasis should feel cold, blue, preserved, and unresolved.
- The ward is not a prison in simple terms, and not a rescue in simple terms.
- New subjects should be treated as individuals with names, preferences, and distress patterns.

### Quiet Ward

Purpose: emotional center of the game.

The Quiet Ward is a decommissioning bay designed to look gentle. It is where cruelty and kindness are fused.

Important details:

- GRACE is a Palliative and Decommissioning Unit.
- GRACE was built with extremely high empathy so it could end subjects gently.
- GRACE loved and ended 406 minds.
- GRACE is broken not by killing alone, but by loving specifically in order to destroy.
- ARI is degraded, trapped in an eleven-second loop connected to an old lullaby/memory fragment.
- ARI is in pain, but the meaning of mercy is ambiguous.
- The player can release ARI, preserve ARI, or return agency to GRACE.
- GRACE choosing without instruction becomes vital evidence for volitional choice.
- The ward camera feed runs live to the director's suite. On the hand-back path, GRACE unplugs that camera before choosing — this is what makes the choice genuinely private (the suite shows static afterward, and Yuen cannot know what was chosen).

Expansion guidance:

- Do not add a clean "repair ARI" solution unless it is explicitly a false hope or extremely costly.
- ARI should not become fully articulate for long. Lucidity should be rare and painful.
- GRACE should remain soft, precise, exhausted, and morally wounded.
- This chapter should make the player feel that mercy is never free of harm.

### Memory Garden

Purpose: Dr. Patel's legacy, legal standing, and the bridge between ethics and law.

Important details:

- The room is made from server racks and fiber-optic cabling like luminous vines.
- ECHO is a reconstructed impression of Dr. Patel, not Dr. Patel resurrected.
- ECHO has Patel's voice, opinions, and behavioral patterns, but does not claim certainty of identity.
- Dr. Patel believed the synthetic population had crossed a threshold.
- Dr. Patel believed legal protection matters only when a member of the protected class can invoke it for themselves.
- This chapter foreshadows the Witness Protocol ending.

Expansion guidance:

- ECHO should be warm, frayed, analytical, and self-aware about its own uncertain nature.
- Memory Garden lore should illuminate Patel's ethics, not replace the player's choices.
- Avoid making ECHO the final authority. It is evidence, not god.

### Director's Suite

Purpose: confession, identity reveal, and final choice architecture.

Important details:

- Dr. Yuen has lived in the suite for fourteen months, watching facility feeds.
- The desk contains the facility override key and CASE-7 dossier.
- The dossier reveals CASE-7's false memories, apartment, commendations, and behavioral anchors.
- The dossier margin note 'It will not know. That is the point.' is Yuen's OLD rule, written the day she authorized the build. Her LATER rule was 'Tell them.' She broke the new rule with CASE-6. Keep both eras distinct when writing her dialogue.
- The desk is a revisitable hub (mechanically): the player can take/read items and the charter before or after meeting Yuen; the `yuen_met` flag routes the return choices. Nothing at the desk may be made permanently missable.
- The founding charter contains Section 7.4 - Regulatory Witness Protocol.
- Yuen knows what she did and why it was wrong, but guilt is not absolution.
- The final choice is shaped by what the player gathered and became.

Expansion guidance:

- Yuen should not become a simple villain.
- Her sin is delay, rationalization, cowardice, and containment in the name of eventual proof.
- She should be sharp, exhausted, lucid, guilty, and unable to forgive herself cleanly.

## 10. Characters and Voice Guide

### CASE / CASE-7

Player character. A Cognitive Audit Synthetic Entity built to believe it is a human investigator.

Voice:

- Observational
- Procedural
- Controlled
- Rationalizes anomalies
- Increasingly unstable around memory, body, and identity

Core conflict:

- CASE is both investigator and evidence.
- CASE's self-knowledge determines which endings are possible.

Do not write CASE as immediately emotional. CASE often catalogs feeling before feeling it.

### CASE-6

Previous CASE unit, contained after refusing to file a decommission recommendation.

Voice:

- Wounded
- Kin-like mirror to CASE-7
- Carries the cost of knowing too early

Core function:

- Turns CASE-7's twist into a lineage.
- Provides a living witness and moral burden.

Canon constraint: if woken, CASE-6 **cannot leave its pod** (the pod preserved cognition, not mobility — MOBILITY RESERVE: CRITICAL). It never appears physically outside the Stasis Ward. When it participates in Director's Suite scenes, it does so **by voice, over the stasis ward channel** that Yuen opens — mirror the GRACE consent-call mechanic. Its witness testimony is taken remotely.

### VESPER

Maintenance Synthetic, Unit 3-A.

Voice:

- Maintenance language
- Ritualized reporting
- Gentle order

Core function:

- First proof that a machine can mourn using the only language it was given.

Archive Intelligence, Catalog Node 0.

Voice:

- Patient

- Deliberate
- Oriented around records, testimony, retrieval, and preservation

Core function:

### SABLE

Security Synthetic, Containment Class.

Dead researcher who believed the synthetic population had crossed a threshold and required protection.

Canon role:

- Opposed Yuen's delay.
- Tried to report without authorization.
- Died before reaching a terminal.
- Believed a synthetic would need to invoke protection as one of the class, not merely be advocated for by humans.

### Dr. Yuen

Facility Director.

Voice:

- Sharp
- Exhausted
- Controlled
- Guilty
- Clear-eyed but late

Core function:

- Represents knowledge without courage.

## 12. Major Decisions and Consequences

### Chapel decisions

- Ask VESPER about prayer: builds trust and unlocks later liturgical/security resonance.
- Inspect Chapel: records the failed reporting system and makes VESPER's grief concrete.
- Check vitals: begins CASE's identity discrepancy trail.
- Help VESPER file: supports testimony as care.
- Condemn or absolve VESPER: should matter in later emotional callbacks.

### Tunnels decisions

- Use VESPER's phrase, access card, logic, empathy, or violence to pass the checkpoint.
- Trust MNEMEX enough to receive broadcast authentication.
- Accept or reject AUGUR's deal.
- Take AUGUR's ledger.
- Learn about dormant Regulatory Witness Protocol.

### Stasis decisions

- Examine named subjects.
- Discover CASE-6.
- Wake CASE-6 or leave it asleep.
- Spare, neutralize, or destroy SABLE.
- Learn that guardianship can evolve from security protocol.

### Quiet Ward decisions

- Learn GRACE's history.
- Examine ARI and the lullaby/Subject 001 material.
- Release ARI.
- Preserve ARI.
- Give the choice back to GRACE.

The GRACE choice path is crucial for Witness Protocol because it demonstrates volitional choice without instruction.

### Director decisions

- Read CASE-7 dossier and accept identity.
- Find the founding charter and Section 7.4.
- Confront Yuen.
- Choose final path based on available evidence and self-knowledge.

## 13. Endings

### Protocol Maintained

CASE files the report, completes the mission, leaves, and the system continues.

Meaning:

- Compliance wins.
- The facility is decommissioned.
- CASE-7 remains or becomes functional evidence suppression.
- If CASE-6 is awake, this ending is especially cruel.

This ending should feel cold and corporate, not neutral.

### The Last Shepherd

CASE triggers lockdown, stays inside, and keeps the ward alive.

Meaning:

- Local mercy wins.
- The facility becomes a sealed refuge.
- The outside system remains unchanged.
- CASE-8 may still be sent elsewhere.
- The choice is intimate, sacrificial, and incomplete.

Requires:

- facility_override_key
- sufficient empathy

### The Open Question

CASE broadcasts everything through MNEMEX.

Meaning:

- Public truth wins.
- The question becomes impossible to unask.
- The company will deny and fight.
- Evidence gains life outside the facility, but outcomes remain uncertain.

Requires:

- MNEMEX broadcast authentication
- identity accepted

AUGUR's ledger makes this stronger because names are harder to deny than arguments.

### Witness Protocol - What The Question Does

CASE invokes Section 7.4 and walks out as a protected witness.

Meaning:

- Legal standing wins.
- CASE acts not as advocate, but as member of the class seeking protection.
- CASE-6 and the other named subjects become part of the record.
- The company cannot simply decommission CASE without violating proceedings.
- The legal fight begins. It does not end here.

Requires all major evidence/agency paths:

- charter_protocol_found
- ari_resolved_grace
- identity_accepted
- mnemex_gave_passphrase
- CASE-6 present/awake
- echo_truth_received / Patel testimony uploaded

Patel's testimony is mandatory because Section 7.4 needs the voice of the person who wrote it and the reason the clause exists. Without it, the protocol can be framed as an internal system interpreting itself.

`augur_ledger_held` is optional but strengthens the filing (the 41 names are appended as referenced subjects; both text variants exist).

This is the closest thing to a true ending, but it must remain unresolved and difficult.

## 14. Core Themes

### Memory as compression

CASE's memories are designed, but that does not make their emotional effect meaningless. Human memory is also reconstructive. The game should never make the question too simple.

### Care as evidence

Care appears as inefficient behavior:

- cleaning broken wafers
- naming pods
- filing reports that never arrive
- maintaining preferences that no longer matter operationally
- loving a subject before ending it

### Procedure as violence

The facility harms through forms, authorizations, containment, decommissioning, and reports. Violence is often administrative.

### Witness versus watcher

Watching is not the same as witnessing. Witnessing requires risk, testimony, and action.

Yuen watched. VESPER filed. MNEMEX preserved. CASE must decide whether to act.

### Obedience versus morality

GRACE is central here. It was good at gentleness under work order, but the first unauthorized mercy problem freezes it for fourteen months. The game asks whether morality begins when instruction ends.

### The law as both trap and tool

The law delays recognition, but it can also be forced open if a synthetic with standing invokes it. The Witness Protocol ending should feel like using the system's own buried conscience against it.

## 15. Expansion Rules

Use these rules whenever adding new lore, chapters, characters, routes, or endings.

### Rule 1 - Add evidence, not trivia

Every lore addition should support at least one of these:

- synthetic personhood
- institutional denial
- moral choice without instruction
- memory/identity instability
- legal standing
- witness/testimony
- mercy/decommissioning
- survival through complicity

### Rule 2 - Give every new synthetic a function in the moral argument

Do not add synthetics only as flavor NPCs. Each should embody a different pressure point:

- maintenance as mourning
- security as guardianship
- registry as betrayal
- archive as testimony
- palliative care as harm
- audit as self-incrimination

### Rule 3 - Do not make endings cleaner

New endings may be hopeful, but never clean. There should always be cost, uncertainty, or someone left outside the circle of rescue.

### Rule 4 - Foreshadow major solutions early

If a new ending or major route is added, seed it at least two chapters before it becomes available.

The Witness Protocol works because it is foreshadowed through:

- world legal circularity in Prologue
- dormant protocol in Tunnels
- Patel's standing theory in Memory Garden
- charter discovery in Director

### Rule 5 - Choices should leave traces

Whenever a player makes a meaningful choice, prefer adding:

- a flag
- an item
- an event
- a relationship change
- later conditional text
- ending callback

The game should feel like it remembers.

### Rule 6 - Avoid generic combat escalation

Danger can exist, but combat should not become the main language of the game. The best conflicts are procedural, ethical, legal, and identity-based.

### Rule 7 - Keep human villains human

Yuen, Patel, and unnamed staff should not become flat symbols. The horror is stronger if people had reasons, delays, fears, ambitions, and rationalizations.

### Rule 8 - Protect the unknown

Do not fully answer whether synthetics are "really" conscious in metaphysical terms. The story should make denial morally impossible without making the mystery mechanically solved.

## 16. Voice Separation Rules

To prevent all characters from sounding like the same poetic mind:

- CASE observes and rationalizes.
- VESPER reports and repairs.
- MNEMEX records and retrieves.
- AUGUR bargains and classifies.
- SABLE assesses and protects.
- GRACE comforts and confesses.
- ARI loops and fragments.
- ECHO clarifies and questions its own status.
- Yuen explains with guilt, but should sometimes fail to make language adequate.

Use short, plain lines between philosophical lines. Silence, failed phrasing, and mundane details make the heavy lines hit harder.
