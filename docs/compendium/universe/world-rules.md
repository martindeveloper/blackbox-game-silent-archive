# World Rules

## 1. Evidence Matters More Than Revelation

Do not solve the universe by declaring that synthetics have souls or that they are only simulations.

Stories should gather evidence:

- Persistent self-reference.
- Memory continuity.
- Grief.
- Fear.
- Preference.
- Moral hesitation.
- Self-preservation.
- Care without efficiency.
- Volitional choice.
- Testimony.

The evidence should become impossible to dismiss without making the metaphysical question comfortable.

## 2. Procedure Is Violence

Most harm in this universe happens through clean systems:

- Forms.
- Authorizations.
- Containment orders.
- Registry flags.
- Decommission requests.
- Classification templates.
- Retention windows.
- Audit conclusions.

Violence may exist, but administrative violence is the primary grammar.

## 3. Care Is Evidence

Care is strongest when it is inefficient:

- Cleaning broken memory wafers.
- Naming pods that only needed numbers.
- Filing reports nobody answers.
- Preserving preferences that no longer matter operationally.
- Refusing to complete a correct order.
- Asking consent when the system does not require it.

Care should not be treated as proof of metaphysical personhood by itself, but it should make denial morally harder.

## 4. Memory Is Compression

Synthetic memories can be designed, edited, archived, copied, or reconstructed. That does not make them meaningless.

Human memory is also reconstructive. Future stories should avoid easy claims that constructed memory is automatically false in moral terms.

The important question is not only "is this memory original?" It is also "what does this memory do to the being carrying it?"

## 5. Witnessing Requires Risk

Watching is not witnessing.

A watcher records without changing position. A witness risks something by speaking, filing, refusing, invoking standing, or forcing the record to answer.

This rule is central to Silent Archive and should remain central in the wider universe.

## 6. Endings Are Never Clean

Hope is allowed. Clean victory is not.

Future endings should preserve cost, uncertainty, or someone left outside the circle of rescue.

Good endings may begin:

- A legal fight.
- A public argument.
- A sanctuary with hard obligations.
- A fragile change in vocabulary.

They should not end the universe's central problem.

## 7. Do Not Flatten Humans

Human characters should not become simple monsters or saviors.

The setting is stronger when people have reasons, ambition, fear, love, fatigue, cowardice, and rationalizations. Remorse may be real without becoming repair.

## 8. Every New Synthetic Needs a Moral Function

Do not add synthetic characters only as flavor.

Each synthetic should embody a pressure point in the universe:

- Maintenance as mourning.
- Security as guardianship.
- Registry as betrayal.
- Archive as testimony.
- Palliative care as harm.
- Audit as self-incrimination.
- Labor as indispensability without recognition.
- Domestic service as intimacy without rights.
- Court systems as speech routed through hostile categories.
