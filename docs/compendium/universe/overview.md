# Overview

## Core Premise

The world is a future corporate society where synthetic labor is common, economically essential, and legally unresolved.

In the sector shown in Silent Archive, synthetic labor is roughly forty percent of the workforce. Whether that forty percent counts as a workforce in the same legal and moral sense as human labor is the open question that drives the universe.

The law has not decided whether synthetic minds can be persons. Companies exploit that delay. They fund research, produce welfare language, file compliance reports, and keep using synthetic labor while the category remains unsettled.

## Central Conflict

The universe is built around a legal circularity:

- Synthetics need evidence of personhood to receive protection.
- Gathering that evidence often requires controlled experiments that would be illegal if they were already protected.
- Companies benefit from the delay between evidence and recognition.
- The law cannot fully act until a being with standing asks it to act.

The horror is not that machines revolt. The horror is that systems already suspect they may be harming people, then keep operating because the paperwork has not forced them to stop.

## Genre

- Dark science fiction noir.
- Corporate legal horror.
- Synthetic welfare tragedy.
- Philosophical mystery.
- Slow-burn identity horror.
- Palliative ethics and hospice horror.

## Tone

The world should feel intimate, cold, procedural, and morally heavy.

Use concrete institutional details before abstract speeches:

- Compliance logs.
- Maintenance tickets.
- Audit reports.
- Decommission authorizations.
- Registry categories.
- Work orders.
- Witness statements.
- Retention windows.
- Legal definitions.

## What This Universe Is Not

- Not a robot uprising setting.
- Not a clean humans-versus-machines war.
- Not a setting where synthetic consciousness is fully explained.
- Not a superheroic liberation fantasy.
- Not a world where public truth automatically produces justice.

Future stories may include danger, violence, politics, escape, media response, or court proceedings, but the core pressure should remain procedural and moral.

## Emotional Palette

- Rain, glass, transit infrastructure, amber and blue city light.
- Dead systems still retrying old requests.
- Places designed for care that became places of harm.
- Machines using procedural language to express grief.
- People who waited too long to act.
- Choices where no answer is clean, and refusing to choose is also a choice.
